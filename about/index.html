About Desoto Water Damage Pros

A 24/7 dispatch line for water damage restoration and mold remediation in DeSoto, Texas and nearby towns.

What Desoto Water Damage Pros does

Desoto Water Damage Pros is a 24/7 dispatch line for water damage restoration and mold remediation in DeSoto, Texas and nearby Dallas County towns. A dispatcher listens to what is happening and sends licensed and insured restoration crews to the job.

The work covers emergency water removal, water mitigation, water damage cleanup, water damage repair, flood damage restoration and mold remediation.

How we work

  • Scope and price are explained before work starts.
  • Crews photograph damage and log moisture readings so your insurer can see what happened.
  • Water damage work follows the published ANSI/IICRC S500 standard and mold work follows S520, which you can ask any company about.
  • Texas licenses mold assessors and remediators through the Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation, so we tell you to ask any mold company for its license.

What we do not do

  • We do not rent drying equipment.
  • We do not do basement waterproofing or foundation repair. If water keeps coming in, you need a drainage or foundation specialist.
  • We do not decide insurance claims, and we cannot promise an insurer will pay.
  • We do not offer plumbing repair. Many callers hire a plumber the same day for the pipe and call us for the water.

How a call becomes a job

You call the 24/7 line and a dispatcher asks about the address, what is wet and whether the source is stopped. That conversation decides which kind of crew is needed, since a burst supply line, a sewer backup and a slow ceiling leak call for different equipment. The dispatcher then explains the next step and what to do while you wait.

On site, the crew checks moisture, photographs the damage and explains the scope and price before work begins. If the situation changes, the scope is discussed again before more work is added. You can ask for the written scope at any point.

What to have ready when you call

  • The address and the nearest cross street.
  • What is wet and roughly how far it has spread.
  • Whether the water is clean, from a drain or from outdoors.
  • Your insurance company name if you have it, so photos and notes are useful for a claim.
  • A callback number.

Why we publish local guides

The blog explains costs, insurance questions and mold basics in plain language. The aim is to help you decide what to do before you call, and to show what a fair written scope looks like. Where a guide cites a city or state rule, it names the source so you can check it yourself.

The pages also say what we cannot do. We cannot promise an insurer will approve a claim, and we do not give medical advice. For those questions the right people are your insurer, the Texas Department of Insurance or a physician.

Standards and rules the work follows

Water damage work is guided by the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard, and mold work by S520. Texas adds its own rules for mold, since mold assessors and remediators are licensed by the Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation. In general, areas under 25 contiguous square feet can be handled by unlicensed people, with exemptions, and larger jobs go to licensed professionals.

Insurance questions are handled by your insurer and regulated by the Texas Department of Insurance. We can supply photos, moisture logs and a written scope, but we do not decide a claim. If a claim is delayed or denied, the department explains your options and timelines on its website.

Who this site is for

The site is written for homeowners, renters and property managers in DeSoto and the nearby towns of Cedar Hill, Duncanville, Lancaster and Red Oak. If you rent, tell your landlord or property manager right away, because they may need to notify their own insurer. If you own a condo, the association documents usually say who is responsible for what.

If your problem is not water damage or mold, such as a roof replacement or a foundation crack, the dispatcher will say so and point you toward the right kind of tradesperson. We would rather tell you that than send a crew that cannot help.

What we ask of you

Please tell the dispatcher the truth about the source of the water and how long it has been there. A leak that has run for weeks is a different job from a pipe that burst this morning, and the crew plans differently for each. Honest details also help your insurer, who will read the same history.

Keep damaged materials until the crew or the adjuster has seen them, unless they are unsafe. Photograph first, then discard. Ask questions at any point, since a clear scope should be easy to explain.
