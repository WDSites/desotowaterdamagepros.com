Emergency Water Removal in DeSoto, TX

Standing water does damage by the hour. Call the 24/7 emergency line, stop the source if it is safe, and keep people and pets out of the water until the crew arrives.

When standing water is an emergency

Water that is still running, still rising or already inside walls is an emergency, and the 24/7 line is open right now. Every hour it sits, water moves into drywall, flooring and framing and the job grows.

While you wait for the crew, stop the source if you can reach it safely. Keep people and pets out of the water, lift valuables off the floor and take photos of the damage before anything is moved.

Stop the source

Close the main water valve or the shutoff under the fixture that failed. If you cannot find it, call the line and the dispatcher will talk you through it.

Power and gas

Turn off power to wet areas only if you can reach the panel without stepping in water. If you smell gas or see sparks, leave and call 911 first.

Lift and protect

Move rugs, small furniture, electronics and paperwork out of the water. Leave heavy furniture for the crew.

Keep clear

Keep children and pets out of standing water. Wear waterproof boots and gloves if you must walk through it.

What emergency water removal involves

Emergency water removal is the fast, physical part of a water loss. The goal is to get standing water out of the building before it spreads into materials that are expensive to replace.

Crews use submersible pumps for deep water and portable extraction units for carpet and hard floors. For larger losses a truck-mounted unit pulls water through a hose from the street. Wet vacuums finish corners, stairs and tight spots.

Once the standing water is out, the crew checks how far moisture traveled. Moisture meters read wet drywall and subfloor, and a thermal imaging camera shows cold, wet patches behind finished surfaces. Those readings decide whether drying can start in place or whether materials have to come out.

Burst pipe water damage

A split pipe can put gallons on the floor every minute, so close the main valve first. Freezing weather is a common trigger in North Texas, and a pipe can split while frozen and only leak after the thaw. Crews extract the water and set drying equipment while a plumber repairs the line. The full guide is on the burst pipe water damage cleanup page, and frozen pipe water damage has its own page.

Water heater and appliance failures

Water heaters, washing machine hoses, dishwasher lines and ice-maker lines cause a large share of indoor flooding, and they often fail while no one is home. Closing the shutoff valve at the unit stops the flow. DeSoto Building Inspections treats water heater replacement as permit work, so ask before the old unit leaves. Details are on the water heater leak cleanup and appliance leak water damage pages.

Crawl spaces, low rooms and garages

Water that cannot drain settles in the lowest place in the structure, which can be a garage, a low room or a crawl space under an older home. Crawl space water is often missed, so look for a musty smell, soft floors and sagging insulation. Extraction in a tight space needs small pumps and a technician working on plastic sheeting. The crawl space water damage page covers it in full.

Electrical, gas and sewage safety first

Water and electricity do not mix, and the risk is not always visible. If water has reached outlets, appliances or the electrical panel, stay out of the room. Turn the power off only if you can reach the panel while standing on dry ground, and never touch a switch while wet.

If you smell gas, see sparks or hear buzzing, leave the building and call 911 and your utility before you call the restoration line. Electrical and gas hazards come first. Restoration can wait a few minutes, and your safety cannot.

Sewage is its own category of hazard. If a toilet, tub or floor drain backs up, keep everyone out of the water and do not try to clean it yourself. The flood damage restoration page explains how crews handle contaminated water.

What affects the cost of emergency water removal

  • Water category. Contaminated water means protective gear, more material removal and more cleaning.
  • Depth and area. More square footage and deeper water take longer to extract.
  • Materials. Carpet and pad, hardwood, laminate and drywall react differently to saturation.
  • Access. A crawl space or a second-floor leak adds time and equipment.
  • What follows. Extraction usually leads to drying, cleanup and repair, and each phase adds to the scope.

Do you handle emergency water removal at night or on weekends in DeSoto?

Yes. The line is open 24 hours a day, including nights, weekends and holidays. The dispatcher asks what is happening and sends a crew based on availability, so ask for the expected arrival when you call.

Night calls get the same equipment as daytime calls. Pumps, extraction units, air movers and dehumidifiers go out on the same truck when the job needs them.

Can I use a wet vacuum myself while I wait?

A household wet vacuum helps with a small spill, but it cannot keep up with an inch of water across a room. Never use one near wet outlets, and never use it on sewage or other contaminated water.

For anything larger than a spill, stay out of the water and call. Pumps and truck-mounted units move far more water than a shop vacuum and finish the job sooner.

How do I know if the water is contaminated?

Water from a clean supply line starts out sanitary, but it picks up contamination as it sits in carpet, pad and insulation. Water from a toilet, a sewer line, a washing machine drain or an outdoor source is already contaminated.

When you cannot tell, treat it as contaminated. Keep children and pets away, wear waterproof boots and gloves and let the crew decide how to handle it.

What happens after the standing water is gone?

Removing the standing water is only the first step. Moisture stays behind in floors, wall cavities and the structure, so drying equipment goes in next to bring it down to a dry standard. That phase is covered on the water mitigation page.

Ruined materials are then removed and cleaned, and rebuilding follows once readings are dry.

Does home insurance cover the cost of removing water?

Often, if the water came from a sudden and accidental source such as a burst pipe. A continuous leak, long-term neglect and flood water are commonly excluded, and flood needs its own policy.

Read your policy, photograph the damage before anything is moved and ask your insurer. Only the insurance company can say what it will pay.
