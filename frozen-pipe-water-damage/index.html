Emergency Frozen Pipe Water Damage Restoration in DeSoto, TX

Texas does not freeze often, which is why a hard freeze catches so many homes off guard. When ice splits a pipe, the real flood starts at the thaw.

Why freezes hurt DeSoto homes so much

North Texas homes are built for heat. According to the National Weather Service in Fort Worth, summer highs are consistently in the 90s and often reach or exceed 100 degrees, so water lines often run through attics, exterior walls and garages with little insulation. A freeze finds the weak spots fast.

The same agency lists the average first freeze at the Dallas-Fort Worth reporting station as November 22 and the average last freeze as March 12. Freezes are normal in winter, but a long one is rare. NOAA described the February 2021 cold outbreak as the coldest air for much of Texas since December 1989, with a Winter Storm Warning covering every Texas county in mid-February.

Water expands as it turns to ice. The pipe does not usually split where the ice sits but just beyond it, where trapped pressure builds. That is why a frozen line can look fine and hold water until the ice melts, then spray or pour once the thaw begins.

First-hour safety steps after a freeze

Shut the water off

Close the main valve before the thaw starts if you can, since a split may begin to flow as soon as the ice melts.

Thaw gently

Use a hair dryer or warm towels on the frozen pipe. Never use an open flame or a torch near a pipe, a wall or insulation.

Mind the power

If water drips near outlets, light fixtures or the panel, keep the power off at a dry panel or call an electrician.

Call early

Call (972) 945-2885 as soon as water appears. Winter storms keep many trades busy, so early calls help.

How crews restore a home after a freeze

The first job is to extract the water. Freeze floods often reach more than one level, because a split in an attic runs through ceilings, walls and floors on the way down. Crews check every room under the affected line, not just where the first stain showed.

Moisture meters and a thermal imaging camera map the wet areas, including wall cavities and attic insulation. Wet insulation and soft drywall are removed, since both stay wet and can grow mold. Dry materials are left in place and dried with air movers and dehumidifiers.

Readings are logged at each visit until the framing and sheathing reach the dry standard. The ANSI/IICRC S500 standard is the reference for that process. Rebuilding follows on the water damage repair side once the structure is dry.

What changes the cost of freeze damage

  • How many levels got wet. Water from the attic reaches more rooms than one from a first floor wall
  • How long water ran. A split caught quickly means less saturation than one that ran overnight
  • Insulation and ceilings. Wet attic insulation and drywall ceilings usually need removal
  • Access. Pipes in tight attics or exterior walls take longer to reach
  • Number of splits. A hard freeze can open several lines at once
  • Drying time. Cold, wet framing and large areas need more equipment days

Insurance and freeze damage

The Texas Department of Insurance says a sudden and accidental release of water is a common covered risk. Freeze damage that bursts a pipe is often treated that way, though policies differ on frozen plumbing and on whether the home was kept heated. Read yours and ask your insurer before you decide.

Photograph the split pipe, the wet areas and the damaged materials. Make only temporary repairs until the adjuster has seen them. Many carriers also expect you to take reasonable steps to protect the home, so keep receipts for emergency work.

Preventing the next freeze loss

  • Know your main valve. Everyone in the house should know where it is and how to close it
  • Open cabinet doors. Let warm air reach pipes under sinks along exterior walls
  • Let faucets drip. A slow drip keeps water moving during a hard freeze
  • Cover exposed lines. Insulate pipes in garages, attics and outside walls
  • Disconnect hoses. Remove garden hoses and shield outdoor spigots
  • Check after a thaw. Look at ceilings and walls for stains, since a split may only leak once the ice melts

Can a pipe be frozen and not leaking yet?

Yes. Ice can plug a line and hold the split closed. The leak often begins only when the ice melts.

If a pipe was frozen, close the main valve before the thaw and check ceilings, walls and floors afterward.

Is a frozen pipe covered by home insurance?

Often, if it burst and caused sudden water damage, but policies differ. Frozen plumbing clauses and heating requirements vary.

Read your policy, document the damage and ask your insurer what applies.

How do I thaw a frozen pipe safely?

Use gentle warmth such as a hair dryer or warm towels. Never use a torch or open flame.

If you cannot find the frozen section or see any leak, close the main valve and call for help.

Do I need a plumber too?

Yes. A plumber replaces the split section. We remove the water and dry the structure.
