Water Damage Insurance Claims in DeSoto, TX

A claim follows a set order and Texas publishes the timelines. Here are the steps, the deadlines to track and the mistakes that sink claims.

The claims process, in the order it happens

Water damage insurance claims in DeSoto, TX follow the same arc as claims anywhere in Texas. You report the loss, the insurer investigates, then it accepts or denies the claim and pays on accepted items. Knowing the Texas timelines helps you notice when a claim is stalling.

The Texas Department of Insurance publishes the timelines, and they are specific enough to put on your calendar the day you file.

Step by step

  1. Stop the loss. Close the valve, tarp the roof or turn off the AC, and keep people safe.
  2. Document. Photograph and video everything before you move it, including the source.
  3. Report promptly. Tell your insurer as soon as possible. Most companies set deadlines, and some policies have a one-year deadline unless you show good cause for delay.
  4. Protect the property. Make only temporary repairs. Permanent repairs before the insurer sees the damage could lead to denial.
  5. Dry the structure. Mitigation records show what was wet and how it was handled.
  6. Keep records. Save receipts, emails and the name of everyone you speak to.

What a restoration crew adds to the file

Crews photograph the damage, take moisture readings and write a scope of work that lists rooms, materials and equipment. The documents help your adjuster see what the crew saw. They do not decide coverage. For losses from a failed hose or a hail storm, see appliance leak water damage and storm water damage cleanup.

The water damage cleanup page explains what is removed and what is kept. Keep photos of discarded materials, because your adjuster may ask to see them.

Texas note: what the state says about timing and repairs

The Texas Department of Insurance tells policyholders to report a claim as soon as possible, to photograph or video damage before repairs and to make only temporary repairs. It also says that for disaster-caused damage you must tell the company in writing at least 61 days before you sue, with exceptions if waiting would cause you to miss the filing deadline.

If you have a claim problem, ask the Texas Department of Insurance how to get help. Keep your policy number, claim number and the adjuster name handy when you call.

Common ways claims go wrong

  • Waiting too long to report.
  • Permanent repairs before the adjuster sees the damage.
  • Throwing away damaged items with no photos.
  • Assuming a flood is covered by a standard policy.
  • Not asking for a written reason when a claim is denied.

A slow leak is a classic trap. Insurers commonly treat a continuous leak as a maintenance issue. Document the discovery date and fix the source quickly.

If the claim is denied or short

Ask for the denial in writing with the reasons. Read the policy language cited. Ask whether the insurer will reconsider with more information, such as moisture logs showing the loss was sudden.

If you cannot resolve it, contact the Texas Department of Insurance. A public adjuster or an attorney can also help, though both involve fees, so ask about terms first. We cannot promise a claim will be approved.

Keeping a clean claim file

Create one folder, paper or digital, for everything connected to the loss. Include photos, videos, receipts, the claim number, the adjuster's name and a log of each call with the date and a short summary. This takes minutes a day and saves a lot of confusion later.

Save damaged items until the adjuster has seen them, unless they are unsafe. If you must discard something, photograph it first. Keep receipts for temporary measures such as tarps, fans or a hotel stay, since these may come up when the claim is settled.

Where do I get help in Texas if my claim stalls?

The Texas Department of Insurance is the state regulator and publishes consumer guides on claims. Ask the department how to file a complaint or get help.

Have your policy number, claim number and adjuster name ready.

How long does an insurer have to respond?

According to the state guide, an insurer must acknowledge your claim within 15 days and accept or deny it within 15 business days of getting all the information it needs. If it needs more time, it can take up to 45 days with a stated reason.

Once it agrees to pay, payment is due within five business days.

Can I hire my own contractor?

Yes. You can hire a restoration company, and the policy may have terms about repairs. Keep the insurer informed and make only temporary repairs until the damage is seen.

Ask your insurer whether it has any requirements.

Does the restoration company file my claim?

No. You file the claim with your insurer. The crew provides photos, moisture logs and a scope of work to support it.
