Mold Removal Cost in DeSoto, TX

Texas splits mold work into assessment and remediation. Here are the line items, what moves the price and how to compare quotes fairly.

Why mold removal cost varies so much

Mold removal cost in DeSoto, TX can range from a small cleaning job to a multi-room project, and the price depends on factors you can learn before the first call. Texas also splits the work into assessment and remediation, which adds line items that homeowners in other states may not see.

No honest company quotes a number without looking. What you can do is understand what is being priced and ask for a written scope.

The line items in a Texas mold job

Not every job needs every line. A small cleanup by a homeowner skips most of them, and a large project includes all of them.

What pushes the cost up or down

  • Area. More affected square footage means more containment, removal and cleaning.
  • Material. Drywall and insulation are cut out and replaced, while framing may be cleaned.
  • Access. Attics, crawl spaces and tight closets slow the work.
  • Moisture source. An active leak must be fixed, and a hidden one costs more to find.
  • Testing. Assessment, labs and clearance are separate from removal.
  • Rebuild. New drywall, insulation, paint and flooring are priced on top.

Ask whether the quote includes the rebuild or stops at removal. Many quotes stop at removal, and the rebuild is a second project.

Separate roles protect you

The state requires a protocol developed by a licensed Mold Assessment Consultant for the remediation work in covered jobs. Ask who is writing the protocol and who is doing the removal, and ask the Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation about the rules on one company doing both.

When the job is done, a licensed remediator can issue a Certificate of Mold Damage Remediation to the property owner. If you are selling or refinancing, ask before the job starts so the paperwork matches what your lender wants.

Texas note: what the state requires licensees to give you

The Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation says licensees must give the client a Consumer Mold Information Sheet before any mold-related activity begins. Assessment samples must go to a licensed lab, and remediation covered by the rules must follow a protocol developed by a licensed Mold Assessment Consultant.

The state also says areas of visible mold of under 25 contiguous square feet can be cleaned by people who are not licensed, with exemptions. For anything larger, ask to see a license before you sign anything.

Insurance and mold costs

The Texas Department of Insurance says mold removal is generally excluded from home policies except to repair damage from a covered risk. If mold followed a covered pipe burst, the cost may be part of that claim. Mold from a long-term leak usually is not.

Check your policy, photograph before cleanup and ask your insurer before you sign a contract. The mold remediation page explains how assessment and removal fit together.

How to compare quotes

  1. Ask for a written scope with areas, materials and steps.
  2. Confirm each company holds the right Texas license for its role.
  3. Ask what is not included, such as rebuild, testing or leak repair.
  4. Ask what happens if more mold is found once walls are open.
  5. Ask for the Consumer Mold Information Sheet before work starts.

If the cause is a leak, water mitigation covers the drying that has to happen as well. We also serve Cedar Hill and Duncanville.

Questions that reveal what a quote includes

Ask whether the price covers containment, air filtration, disposal and the final check. Some quotes list only labor, which makes them look lower than they are. Ask who does the testing and whether that person is separate from the crew doing the work.

Request the scope in writing with the rooms, square footage and materials to be removed. Compare quotes line by line rather than by the bottom number. A clear scope also gives your insurer something concrete to read if coverage applies.

Does Texas require testing before mold removal?

Remediation covered by state rules must follow a protocol developed by a licensed Mold Assessment Consultant, so assessment comes first for covered jobs. Small jobs under 25 contiguous square feet by an unlicensed person have exemptions.

Ask the Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation if you are unsure which category your job falls in.

Can the same company test and remove the mold?

Ask the Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation about the rules on one company doing both on the same project. Many homeowners prefer separate companies for independent clearance.

Whatever you choose, ask each company for its license.

Is clearance testing worth the cost?

It adds a second check, which can matter for a sale or loan. Lenders and buyers may ask for it.

We cannot promise any party will accept a document, so ask what they require first.

Will removing mold fix the problem if the leak continues?

No. Mold returns to wet material. Fix the water source and dry the structure first.
