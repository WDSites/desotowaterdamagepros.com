Commercial Water Damage Restoration in DeSoto, TX

When water gets into a business, every hour counts. This page explains what our crews can do, what we hand to others, and what to have ready when you call.

What we can do for a commercial water loss

Water behaves the same way in a shop or an office as it does in a house. It follows floors, soaks walls and ceilings, and keeps moving into materials. Our crews remove standing water, find wet materials with moisture meters and thermal imaging, and dry the structure with air movers and dehumidifiers.

We handle extraction, drying, cleanup of wet materials and moisture monitoring. We can also document wet areas with photographs and drying logs. We do not manage your business, move your inventory, repair your equipment or replace systems. Rebuilding work, such as ceilings, walls and floors, is a separate step after the structure is dry.

Every building is different, and size and layout decide how the work goes. A large open floor with a slab dries differently from a suite with several interior walls and a drop ceiling. The dispatcher asks about the space and what failed, so the crew arrives with the right kind of equipment.

First-hour safety steps for a building

Protect people

Move staff and visitors away from wet floors and from ceilings that sag or drip.

Stop the source

Close the nearest valve or the main. If the source is shared, notify the building manager.

Electrical caution

Keep the power off in wet areas and do not use equipment in standing water. Call an electrician for the panel.

Protect what matters

Lift records, devices and stock off the floor if you can do it safely, then call (972) 945-2885.

How we dry a commercial space

The crew starts with the water. Pumps and extraction units remove standing water from floors. Then the crew uses moisture meters to map walls, floors and ceilings and a thermal imaging camera to find wet zones behind finishes. Readings are recorded so the loss can be tracked.

Wet ceiling tiles, insulation and soft drywall are removed. Carpet, pad and floor coverings that cannot be dried are lifted. Air movers and dehumidifiers go in, and readings are logged at each visit until materials meet the dry standard. The ANSI/IICRC S500 standard is the reference for this method.

If water reached a shared wall or a neighboring unit, tell the property manager. We can describe what is wet and what we find, but access and approvals belong to the owner or manager. Drying is covered in more detail on the structural drying and dehumidification page.

What changes the cost of commercial water damage work

  • Square footage. Larger areas need more equipment and more days
  • Floor and ceiling finishes. Carpet tile, sealed concrete and drop ceilings are handled differently
  • Source and category. Clean supply water and sewage lead to different work
  • Layout. Many small rooms mean more setup than one open floor
  • Access. Occupied buildings may limit hours and equipment placement
  • What follows. Repairs and rebuild are a separate scope

Insurance, owners and tenants

Who pays depends on the lease and the policies. Commercial property policies differ from homeowner policies, and tenants and owners may each carry coverage. The Texas Department of Insurance is the state regulator for insurance, and your agent or the policy language answers coverage questions.

Photograph the damage before cleanup, note what was wet and when, keep receipts, and notify your insurer and the property manager early. Per the Texas Department of Insurance, temporary repairs are fine before the adjuster visits, and permanent ones should wait.

Mistakes to avoid in a commercial loss

  • Waiting for the owner or the insurer first. Water keeps spreading while approvals are pending
  • Leaving wet ceiling tiles up. They hold water and can fall
  • Running the building as normal. Wet floors and wiring are hazards
  • Skipping moisture checks. Hidden water causes odors and mold
  • Covering damp walls. Finishes trap moisture
