Condo Insurance Coverage for Water Damage in DeSoto, TX

In a condo, two policies can respond to one leak. Here is how the layers usually divide and what to do first.

Two policies, one leak: who covers what in a condo

Condo insurance coverage for water damage in DeSoto, TX usually involves two layers. The association carries a policy on the building, and you carry your own policy for your unit and belongings. Which layer pays depends on where the water came from and what your governing documents say.

There is no single rule, because associations write their documents differently. Some cover the walls and fixtures inside your unit, and others stop at bare walls and leave interior finishes to you. Your declaration, bylaws and policy decide.

What a unit owner policy often includes

  • Personal belongings, such as furniture, clothes and electronics.
  • Interior finishes or improvements you own, depending on the documents.
  • Additional living expenses if you cannot stay in the unit.
  • Personal liability if your leak damages a neighbor.
  • Loss assessment coverage, which can help with charges the association passes on to owners.

Ask your agent which of these your policy has, and what your deductible is. Also ask whether you have coverage for the association deductible if the association files a claim and charges owners.

Steps to take after a leak

  1. Stop the water if you can reach the valve and tell the property manager at once.
  2. Photograph and video the damage and the source.
  3. Contact your insurer and the association to report the loss.
  4. Ask which party will handle drying and which will handle rebuilding.
  5. Keep receipts for hotel stays or other added costs.

Drying should not wait for a coverage decision. Water in a shared wall can spread to other units, so telling the manager early helps everyone. See emergency water removal for what crews do first.

DeSoto note: permits and association approval both apply

DeSoto Building Inspections says a permit is required to alter or repair a building and to replace electrical, plumbing or mechanical systems, and that work must pass inspection before it is concealed. A condo owner doing repairs inside the unit still deals with the city, and usually with the association as well.

Association rules often require approval for floor, wall or plumbing changes and for who may work in shared systems. Ask your manager before work starts, and call the city at 972-230-9610 about permits.

Mold and related claims

The Texas Department of Insurance says mold removal is generally excluded from home policies except to repair damage from a covered risk. A unit policy may follow the same pattern. If mold appears after a covered leak, tell your insurer before cleanup.

Drying records matter. Moisture readings and photos show how quickly the unit was dried. The water mitigation page explains what those records contain.

Questions to ask

  • What do the association documents say about interior finishes?
  • Does my policy have loss assessment coverage and what is the limit?
  • What is the association deductible and who pays it?
  • Who is responsible if my fixture damages a neighbor?
  • Do I have separate flood coverage if the unit is at ground level?

Check your policy and ask your insurer and association. We cannot decide coverage. Crews also serve Duncanville and Red Oak.

Questions to ask before you file

Start by finding the association documents and your own policy side by side. The master policy usually covers the building structure, while your unit policy covers contents, improvements and loss assessments. Where one ends and the other begins depends on the wording, so read both before you assume anything.

Call the management office the same day and ask who handles the common plumbing, who hires the mitigation company and how the deductible is divided. Keep copies of every email. If water came from the unit above you, ask for the name of that owner's insurer in writing.

Do DeSoto condo owners need city permits for repairs inside a unit?

Yes, when the work alters or repairs the building or replaces plumbing, electrical or mechanical systems. DeSoto Building Inspections requires a permit for that work and an inspection before it is concealed.

Cosmetic work may differ, so call 972-230-9610. Ask your association for approval as well.

Who pays if my leak damages my neighbor?

That depends on the cause and your policy. Liability coverage in a unit owner policy can respond to damage you cause, while the association policy may handle building damage.

Report the loss to your insurer and the property manager, and keep records.

Should I wait for the association before drying my unit?

No. Drying should start quickly, even while coverage is sorted out. Waiting lets moisture spread and can lead to mold.

Tell the manager, take photos and arrange drying. Ask the association which vendor to use if rules require one.

Is flood damage covered for a ground-floor condo?

Flood is usually excluded from standard policies and needs a separate flood policy. The Texas Department of Insurance notes most flood policies have a 30-day waiting period.

Ask your agent and the association what flood coverage exists.
