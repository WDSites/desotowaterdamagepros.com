Where Wolf Creek Estates sits

Wolf Creek Estates is a residential subdivision in DeSoto's 75115 ZIP code. Listing records place homes on streets such as Wolf Drive, Keswick Drive, Sabona Drive and Ruffian Road. Local TV coverage of the 2022 storm also described a subdivision near farmland off South Cockrell Hill Road.

We have not confirmed an official boundary, so treat this as an approximate description. If you are unsure whether your street belongs to the subdivision, your deed, your HOA or the city can tell you.

What the August 2022 storm showed

On Monday, August 22, 2022, torrential rain left at least 19 homes in a DeSoto neighborhood with water inside, according to a city statement reported by NBC DFW. The coverage named Wolf Creek Estates as one affected area. After inspections, the city said some homes had no damage or only minor damage.

The city said the flooding came from excess water entering a drainage system that was already overwhelmed. Runoff from the neighborhood and an adjacent vacant field drained to a detention area. The city's 2026 Drainage Master Plan, prepared by an engineering firm, also cites flooded homes in the Wolf Creek neighborhood during that storm, which brought 6.8 inches of rain in 24 hours.

One storm is not a pattern. The same plan states that the city has not historically received frequent flooding complaints, and it does not label any home as flood-prone. We mention the event because it shows how fast heavy rain can outrun a drainage system, not to say your house will flood.

Safety steps before you touch anything

Stay out of moving water

Do not wade through water near outlets, cords or appliances. Rain water that has crossed lawns, streets and fields can carry soil, fuel and waste.

Power

Do not touch the breaker panel while standing in water. If you smell gas or see sparks, leave and call 911.

Protect what is dry

Lift small items to higher shelves and photograph the water line and damaged items when it is safe to do so.

Call before you clean

Call (972) 945-2885 before pulling out materials, so we can talk through what to keep as a record.

What storm water cleanup involves

Water that enters from outside is treated as contaminated until the source is known. ANSI/IICRC S500 is the reference standard we use to describe water categories. Crews pump out standing water, remove porous materials that cannot be cleaned, then set air movers and dehumidifiers and take moisture readings until the structure reads dry.

Carpet pad, baseboards and the lower part of drywall are the usual first losses when rain water reaches a ground floor. Our flood damage restoration page covers extraction and removal. For the drying stage, see structural drying.

Insurance and documentation

Coverage depends on how the water got in and on your policy. The Texas Department of Insurance explains that flood is commonly excluded from standard homeowners policies, and that flood policies often have a waiting period before they take effect. Photos, moisture readings and a written scope can help you describe the work, but we cannot say what any insurer will pay.

Was Wolf Creek Estates flooded in 2022?

Local TV reported that torrential rain on August 22, 2022 left at least 19 homes in a DeSoto neighborhood with water inside and named Wolf Creek Estates as an affected area. The city later said some homes had no damage or only minor damage.

Is every home in Wolf Creek Estates in a flood zone?

No. Neighborhood-specific FEMA flood-zone status not verified. A flood zone applies to mapped areas, not to a subdivision by name. Use the FEMA Flood Map Service Center with your own address.

What should I do first if rain water enters my house?

Stay out of the water if outlets or cords are nearby, stop any water source you can reach safely and photograph the damage. Then call (972) 945-2885 before you start removing materials.

Does homeowners insurance cover water that comes in from the street?

It depends on the cause and on your policy. Flood is commonly excluded from standard homeowners coverage, so ask your insurer how it classifies rain water entering from outside.

How long does drying take after storm water?

Many jobs take a few days, but wet subfloors, insulation and thick materials can take longer. Moisture readings decide when drying is finished, not the calendar.
