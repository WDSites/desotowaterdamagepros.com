Will Home Insurance Cover Water Damage in DeSoto, TX?

Coverage turns on the cause of the water. Here is how Texas guidance separates sudden leaks from gradual ones and why flood is its own policy.

The short answer: it depends on how the water got there

Will home insurance cover water damage in DeSoto, TX? The answer turns on the cause. According to the Texas Department of Insurance, a sudden and accidental release of water is a common covered risk, while a continuous water leak and flood are typically not covered. Pipe failures are covered in more detail on the burst pipe water damage cleanup page, and drain backups on the sewage backup cleanup page.

That single distinction explains most denied claims. A pipe that bursts on Tuesday is a sudden event. A fitting that dripped for six months behind a cabinet is a continuous leak. Your adjuster will look for how long the water was present.

Mold and related damage

The Texas Department of Insurance says mold removal is generally excluded except to repair damage from a covered risk. If mold follows a covered pipe burst, it may be part of the claim. If mold grew because of a long leak, it is usually not.

This is another reason to dry fast. Drying records show the crew took steps to limit mold. The water mitigation page explains what those records look like.

What to do in the first hours

  1. Stop the water and keep people safe.
  2. Photograph and video the damage before anything is moved.
  3. Make only temporary repairs, such as closing a valve or tarping a roof. Texas guidance warns against permanent repairs before the insurer sees the damage.
  4. Report the loss to your insurer as soon as possible.
  5. Call a restoration company to dry the structure and document the loss.

How we can help and where we stop

Crews photograph the damage, record moisture readings and write a scope of work. That paperwork gives your adjuster the same picture the crew saw. We do not decide coverage, and we cannot promise that an insurer will approve or pay a claim.

DeSoto note: storms, flood zones and separate flood coverage

In August 2022, local news reported that the city counted at least 19 homes with water inside after torrential rain overwhelmed drainage in parts of DeSoto. Rainwater and drainage backup are the kind of event standard homeowners policies often exclude, so a flood policy or an endorsement may matter.

The Texas Department of Insurance says most flood policies have a 30-day waiting period, so buying cover on the day a storm is forecast does not help. Check your address on the FEMA Flood Map Service Center, and ask your agent about flood coverage even if the map shows low risk.

Questions to ask your insurer

  • Does my policy cover sudden and accidental water discharge, and what is my deductible?
  • Is there an exclusion for continuous or repeated seepage?
  • Do I have coverage for sewer or drain backup?
  • Is mold covered if it follows a covered loss?
  • What deadlines apply for reporting and for filing a claim?

If the loss is happening now, start with emergency water removal. For the rebuild after drying, see water damage repair. Crews also serve Cedar Hill and Lancaster.

Reading your own policy before you call

Pull out the declarations page and the full policy, not just the summary. Look for the words sudden, accidental, seepage and continuous, because they decide most water claims. Note your deductible and any separate limit for water or mold.

Then write down a timeline of what happened, including when you first saw the damage and when you stopped the water. Photographs with dates help. If something in the wording is unclear, ask the insurer to explain it in writing and keep the reply with your claim file.

If a DeSoto storm floods my garage, does my homeowners policy pay?

Usually not for flood. The Texas Department of Insurance says most home policies do not cover damage caused by floods, and flood coverage is a separate policy.

Wind and hail damage that lets rain in can be a different story, so report the loss and ask your insurer how it classifies the event.

Is a slow leak under my sink covered?

Often not. Policies commonly exclude a continuous water leak, and insurers may call it a maintenance issue. A sudden failure of the same line is a different category.

Fix the leak and document it. Ask your insurer what applies.

Does my insurer have a deadline for me to report?

Most companies set deadlines, and some policies have a one-year deadline unless you can show good cause for the delay. The state advises telling your company as soon as possible.

Read your policy for the exact timeframe.

Can I start repairs before the adjuster sees the damage?

Make only temporary repairs and photograph everything first. The state warns that permanent repairs before the insurer sees the damage could lead to denial.

Dry the structure, but keep ruined materials or photos for the adjuster.
