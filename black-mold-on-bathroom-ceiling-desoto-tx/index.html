Black Mold on a Bathroom Ceiling in DeSoto, TX

Bathroom ceiling mold usually comes from steam, weak ventilation or a hidden leak. Here is how to tell which one you have and what to fix.

Why bathroom ceilings grow dark spots

Black mold on a bathroom ceiling in DeSoto, TX usually has a plain cause. Hot showers fill the room with steam, the steam meets a cooler ceiling and condenses, and a ceiling that stays damp feeds growth. A weak or blocked exhaust fan makes it worse.

A less obvious cause is a leak. A drip from the shower valve, tub drain or an upstairs fixture can wet the ceiling from above. If the spots are in one place and keep returning, look for a leak before blaming steam.

Steam or leak? A quick check

Telling condensation from a leak

A moisture meter helps confirm which one you have. If the reading is high only after showers, it is condensation. If it stays high, there is a leak.

Cleaning a small patch

  1. Run the fan and open a window, and keep other rooms closed off.
  2. Wear gloves, eye protection and a mask suited to the work.
  3. Wipe the ceiling with detergent and water on a damp cloth, avoiding soaking the surface.
  4. Dry the ceiling with a clean towel and let it air dry fully.
  5. Prime with a stain-blocking, mold-resistant primer and repaint with bathroom paint.

If the ceiling is drywall and the growth keeps returning after cleaning, the section may be wet inside. Cut-out and replacement is the lasting fix.

Fix the ventilation

  • Run the exhaust fan during showers and for about 20 minutes afterward.
  • Make sure the fan vents outside through a duct, not into the attic.
  • Check that the duct is not crushed or disconnected.
  • Replace a loud, weak fan with a properly sized one.
  • Wipe down the ceiling and walls after steamy showers if the fan is weak.

An exhaust fan that vents into an attic dumps all that steam above the ceiling. That alone can cause damage that looks like a leak.

DeSoto note: fan replacement and electrical permits

If fixing the ventilation means replacing an exhaust fan or running new wiring, DeSoto Building Inspections says a permit is required to erect, install, alter or replace electrical systems regulated by the adopted codes. Work involving electrical systems must be inspected before it is concealed.

A simple like-for-like fan swap may be treated differently from new wiring, so call the department at 972-230-9610 before you start. The office is at 211 E. Pleasant Run Rd., open Monday through Friday from 8 a.m. to 5 p.m.

Texas rules if the area is larger

The Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation says areas of visible mold of less than 25 contiguous square feet can be cleaned by people who are not licensed, with exemptions. A large patch, or any testing, needs licensed professionals. Questions about symptoms belong to a physician.

The state advises that mold removal is generally excluded from home insurance except to repair damage from a covered risk. If a hidden leak was the cause, ask your insurer before you open the ceiling and photograph the damage first.

Why bathroom ceilings grow mold first

Steam rises and condenses on the coolest surface in the room, which is often the ceiling. A fan that is undersized, ducted into the attic or switched off right after a shower leaves that surface damp for hours. Painted drywall and a thin film of soap residue give growth plenty to feed on.

Check the fan before you scrub anything. Hold a tissue under it while it runs and see whether it holds. Then look above the ceiling for wet insulation or a stained roof deck, because a slow plumbing or roof leak can look exactly like a ventilation problem from below.

Small habits help too. Wipe condensation from the ceiling and walls after long showers, leave the door open to let humid air spread out and let the fan run for a while afterward. These steps cost almost nothing and slow regrowth considerably.

How long should I run the bathroom fan?

Run it during the shower and for roughly 20 minutes afterward, or until the mirror clears and the room feels dry.

A fan on a timer switch makes this automatic.

Is a mold-resistant paint enough to stop the spots?

No. Mold-resistant paint helps keep a dry surface clean but cannot solve a damp ceiling.

Fix ventilation and any leaks first, then paint.

When should I call a professional for a bathroom ceiling?

Call if the ceiling is soft or sagging, if spots return after cleaning, if the patch is large or if a leak is behind it.

A moisture meter and thermal imaging can show what is wet above the ceiling.
