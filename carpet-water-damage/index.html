Carpet Water Damage Cleanup in DeSoto, TX

Carpet and pad hold a lot of water. Whether they can be saved depends on what kind of water it was and how fast the drying starts.

Why carpet is the first thing to go wrong

Carpet is a sandwich. A surface layer of fibers sits on a backing, and below it lies a foam or fiber pad. The pad is the sponge. It holds many times more water than it seems, and it keeps releasing that water into the subfloor long after the surface feels dry.

The type of water decides the plan. Clean supply water from a failed line may allow the carpet to be lifted, the pad removed and the carpet dried and reinstalled. Water from a washer drain, a toilet overflow or a storm is not clean, and the carpet usually comes out.

In August 2022, local news reports citing the city counted at least 19 homes with water inside after heavy rain in DeSoto, a reminder of how much carpet gets wet in a storm.

DeSoto buys its drinking water from the City of Dallas, according to city water information, so many losses begin with clean water from a supply line. That is the best case. Carpet can often be saved when extraction and drying start in the first day or two, and the sooner the pad comes out, the better the odds.

First-hour safety steps for wet carpet

Stop the source

Close the valve or stop the leak. Carpet cannot dry while water is still coming.

Lift items

Move furniture and boxes. Place foil or plastic under legs, since wood stain bleeds into wet carpet.

Mind the power

Keep the power off in rooms where water reaches outlets and avoid wet cords.

Call early

Call (972) 945-2885. Wet carpet starts to smell in a day or two.

How crews restore wet carpet

The crew first extracts water from the carpet using weighted extraction tools, which pull water out of the fibers and the pad. Then moisture meters check the pad, the tack strip, the baseboard and the subfloor. The wet pad is usually removed, because it dries slowly and can hold contamination.

If the carpet can be saved, it is folded back or lifted along one wall, and air movers and dehumidifiers dry the carpet and the subfloor. The carpet is cleaned and reinstalled or restretched. If it is contaminated, it is cut up and removed. Readings are logged until materials meet the dry standard, in line with ANSI/IICRC S500.

Odors can remain after drying, so deodorizing and cleaning follow when needed. Related work is covered on water damage cleanup, and the drying method is on structural drying and dehumidification.

What changes the cost of carpet water damage work

  • Water category. Contaminated water means removal, while clean water may allow drying
  • Pad. Replacing pad is routine, and replacing carpet is a bigger step
  • Area. More square footage means more extraction and drying
  • Subfloor. Wet subfloor adds drying
  • Stairs and tight rooms. Access slows the work
  • Reinstalling. Stretching and reinstalling is a separate task

Insurance and wet carpet

The Texas Department of Insurance says a sudden and accidental release of water is a common covered risk, while a continuous leak and flood are typically not covered. Carpet damaged by a burst line is often covered. Carpet damaged by outside flood water usually needs a flood policy, which has a 30-day waiting period.

Photograph the carpet and keep a swatch of any carpet that is removed. The Texas Department of Insurance recommends temporary repairs only until an adjuster has seen what happened.

Mistakes to avoid with wet carpet

  • Using a household carpet cleaner. It adds more water and does not reach the pad
  • Leaving the pad in. It stays wet and grows odors
  • Covering with plastic. Moisture is trapped
  • Walking on wet carpet. The backing stretches and stains spread
  • Pulling it up yourself. You may spread contamination
